                                                  1. Until

Until is used to indicate time.

It tells us that something continues up to a particular time.

Examples

I will wait until you come.

She stayed there until evening.

Don't leave until I return.

                                                  2. Unless

Unless is used to express a condition.

It generally means:

unless = if...not

Examples

You will not pass unless you study.

Meaning:

You will not pass if you do not study.

IMPORTANT:Should not use negative when unless is used .
❌ Unless you don't study, you will fail.

✅ Unless you study, you will fail.


                                                    3. If

If is mainly used for:

A. Condition

If you study hard, you will pass.

If it rains, we will stay home.

Here while using if as condition "will" should not be used
Yes — **for a normal future condition, we generally do NOT use “will” in the `if` clause.** This is an important Rule 6 point. ✅

### ❌ Don't use:

> If you **will study** hard, you will pass.

### ✅ Use:

> If you **study** hard, you will pass.

The basic pattern is:

> **If + Present Simple, ... will + V1**

Examples:

* If it **rains**, we **will stay** home.
* If she **studies** hard, she **will pass**.
* If you **work** hard, you **will succeed**.
* If they **come** tomorrow, we **will meet** them.

### Why?

The **if-clause** gives the **condition**, while the other clause gives the **future result**.

> **If you study** → condition
> **you will pass** → result

So:

> **If + present → will + V1**

### But there is an important exception ⚠️

**Will can appear after if when “will” has its normal meaning of willingness, insistence, or refusal—not simply future time.**

For example:

> If you **will listen** to me, I can explain it.

Here **will listen** means **“are willing to listen.”**

So don't memorize:

> ❌ **“Will can NEVER be used after if.”**

Instead memorize:

> ⭐ **For a normal future condition, don't use “will” in the if-clause.**

**If + present simple → will + V1**

This is the version you should put in your Rule 6 notes.




B. Doubt / uncertainty

If can also be used when we are uncertain about something.

I don't know if he will come.

She asked if I was ready.

                                                   4. Whether

Whether is used when there is doubt, uncertainty, or alternatives.

Examples

I don't know whether he will come.

I don't know whether she is telling the truth.

I don't know whether or not he will come.

<-------Whether vs If-------->

Both can sometimes be used to express uncertainty:

I don't know if he will come. ✅

I don't know whether he will come. ✅

But whether is preferred when there is an explicit alternative:

I don't know whether he will come or not. ✅

We have to decide whether to stay or leave. ✅


                                                    5. That

That can be used as a conjunction to introduce a clause, especially after verbs of thinking, saying, knowing, believing, etc.

Examples

I know that he is honest.

She said that she was tired.

I believe that he will come.

He thinks that she is right.
 

<------------diff b/w that and whether----------->

I have few doubts that he will come.
I have a few doubts whether he will come.

1. "Few doubts" implies certainty -> Use "that"
The Logic: The word "few" has a negative meaning. It means "almost none" or "not many."
The Result: If you have almost no doubts, it means you are certain that he will show up.
The Rule: When a sentence expresses certainty or a positive belief, it must be followed by that.

2. "A few doubts" implies uncertainty -> Use "whether"
The Logic: The phrase "a few" has a positive meaning. It means "some" or "a small number".
The Result: If you have some doubts, it means you are uncertain or hesitant about his arrival.
The Rule: When a sentence expresses doubt, hesitation, or uncertainty, it must be followed by whether or if.
